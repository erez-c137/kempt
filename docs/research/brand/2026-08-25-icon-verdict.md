# Icon verdict - candidates panel, 2026-08-25

Three independent judges (Breeze/KDE icon designer, brand designer, skeptical Fedora KDE user)
plus the controller reviewed six candidates in `candidates/`: two comb variants (Gemini cream
comb on slate; ChatGPT white comb on bright blue) and a 2x2 grid of generic update concepts
(Tux in refresh ring, shield + arrow, monitor + package, gear + arrow).

## Consensus

- **Direction: the comb.** Unanimous. It is the only concept that says "kempt" rather than
  "update", and the only one with no Breeze collision at full size.
- **Base: the Gemini variant** (3 of 4). Better spine-to-teeth proportion (36/64 vs 44/56, which
  drifts toward Breeze's `measure` ruler), and an ownable two-colour palette: slate #1F5885 with
  cream #F2F0E3 measures 6.3:1 contrast, higher than white on bright blue (5.7:1), and is absent
  from every store grid, where the bright blue reads as a KDE system component.
- **The four grid concepts are discarded.** Every one duplicates an existing Breeze icon
  (`view-refresh`, `security-high`, `computer`, `configure`), and Tux is a third-party mascot.
- **None of the six is shippable as drawn.** All are opaque rasters, and both combs collapse into
  a single bar at 16 and 22 px (about 17 hairline teeth = 1.0 px pitch on a 16 px canvas). A
  lone bar reads as a MINUS sign, the worst possible misread for an updater. A 5-tooth redraw
  resolves crisply at the same sizes: a drawing failure, not a concept failure.

## Redraw specification (binding for the SVGs)

Full-colour app icon (store, GitHub avatar, README):
- Slate tile #1F5885, cream comb #F2F0E3, two flat colours only, no gradient, bevel or shadow.
- True squircle with transparent corners (not a soft octagon); survives a circle crop.
- Comb fills about 72% of the tile width and 55% of its height (today: 78% x 26%, so it reads
  as a detail next to the wordmark).
- 5 teeth (4 as fallback if 125%/150% scaling merges them); tooth width equal to gap width;
  spine-to-teeth ratio kept at 36/64; square tooth tips (max 0.5 px radius at small sizes).

Panel glyph (`kempt-symbolic.svg`, monochrome, Breeze style):
- Pure `currentColor` fill, no tile, so it inherits every colour scheme.
- Two hand-hinted drawings, 16 px and 22 px, never one scaled: 16 px target about 14 w x 10 h
  (3 px spine, 6-7 px teeth, 5 teeth at 2 px stroke / 1 px gap = 14 px exactly).
- Keep clear of Breeze `actions/22/measure` (ruler): proportion + square tips do this.
- Reserve the bottom-right quadrant for the count badge: shorten the last tooth or shift the
  glyph 1 px up-left in the 22 px drawing.
- The optional down-chevron accent is for 48 px and above only.

Renders that back this up live with the judges' scratch output for the session and are
regenerated by the legibility sheet in `candidates/legibility/`.

## Ends and tooth-count decision (2026-08-25, later)

**Flush ends, six teeth, and a comb that is wider than it is tall.** This section supersedes the
redraw specification above wherever the two disagree: the tooth count, the footprint and the
proportions below are the binding ones for the full-colour icon.

Two rounds got here. The first fixed the ends: the redraw shipped with a 20 px spine overhang at
each side, so the comb ended on a bar rather than on a tooth, and a spine running past its teeth
reads as a lintel over five columns. Flush ends - the spine stopping exactly where the outermost
teeth stop - fixed that. But paying for the lost width with height produced a 144 x 160 comb, and
the founder's read of it was correct and fatal: *it doesn't resemble a comb when it's as tall as
it is wide*. Combs are wide. A taller-than-wide comb reads as a gate, a portcullis, a barcode -
anything but the object.

**The sixth tooth is what released the constraint.** The trap was framed as any two of three: a
16 px tooth pitch, flush ends, and enough width. With **five** teeth it really is a trap, because
five teeth at pitch 16 span only 144 px, and a comb tall enough to carry visual weight next to
144 px of width is roughly square. Adding a sixth tooth buys 32 px of width without touching the
pitch and without loosening the ends. The footprint goes to 176 px, the height drops back to
128 px, and all three conditions hold at once.

Final geometry, all coordinates multiples of 8:

| | |
|---|---|
| Comb | x 40-216, y 64-192 - **176 x 128** = 68.75% x 50% of the 256 tile |
| Aspect | 1.375 : 1 landscape (was 0.9 : 1, taller than wide) |
| Spine | y 64-112, 48 px = 37.5%, full 176 px width, top corners r=8 |
| Teeth | y 112-192, 80 px = 62.5%, square tips |
| Teeth x | 40-56, 72-88, 104-120, 136-152, 168-184, 200-216 (6 x 16 + 5 x 16 = 176) |

Tooth pitch 16 is still the term that cannot give, and it is why the count went to six rather than
the footprint being stretched over five. At 32 px every edge lands on an integer: each tooth is
exactly 2 px of solid cream, each gap exactly 2 px of solid slate, and a row through the teeth
measures `240 240 | 88 88` six times over with not one antialiased half-tone in it. The spine ends
land hard on the same two columns as the outer teeth. 64 px is exact for the same reason (4 px
teeth, 4 px gaps, zero half-tones). 48 px is not a clean divisor of 256 and puts the tooth edges on
half-pixels, but the resulting edge pixel is identical on both sides of all six teeth, so the comb
stays even - and that is unchanged from the previous drawing, not a cost of this one. Stretching
the pitch instead would grey the teeth out at exactly the size the panel uses.

The comb's far corner now sits 108.8 px from centre, comfortably inside the 128 px inscribed
circle, so the drawing still survives a circle crop.

**The symbolic glyphs keep FIVE teeth.** `kempt-symbolic.svg` (22 px) and `kempt-symbolic-16.svg`
are unchanged. At those sizes a tooth is 2 px with a 1 px gap, so five teeth already consume the
full 14 px glyph width; a sixth would need either a 16 px glyph that no longer clears the count
badge, or a 1 px tooth, which is the hairline failure the whole redraw existed to fix. The
tension between the two drawings is real but correct: the app icon is read as an object at 128 px
and above, where the sixth tooth is what sells the object, and the panel glyph is read as a mark
at 16 and 22 px, where legibility outranks fidelity to the big drawing. Both stay flush-ended,
which is the property that actually carries across the sizes.

The distinction from Breeze `actions/22/measure` (the ruler) is unaffected, and the wider
silhouette helps rather than hurts: a ruler is a hollow outlined box with short ticks hanging
inside it, while this is a solid spine with long teeth hanging outside it and open at the bottom.
Nothing in that comparison ever rested on the ends being ragged or on the comb being narrow.
